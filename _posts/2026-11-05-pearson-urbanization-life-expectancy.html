--- layout: post title: "Do more urbanized countries live longer? A Pearson correlation" date: 2026-11-05 09:20:00 +0500 ---

This is the third post in a small series on the Gapminder country data from the Data Analysis Tools course (213 countries, one row per country). The earlier posts compared life expectancy and internet use across income groups. This one looks at two quantitative variables directly.

Question and variables

Question: is the share of a country's population living in cities linked to how long people live there?

  • Explanatory variable: urbanrate, urban population as a percentage of the total.
  • Response variable: lifeexpectancy, life expectancy at birth in years.

Hypotheses. H0: there is no linear relationship between the two variables (the population correlation is 0). H1: there is a linear relationship. Significance level: 0.05.

Load the data and draw the scatterplot

I keep the 173 countries that have all four series variables (income, life expectancy, internet use, urbanization), the same sample as in the other posts. The file is the Gapminder course file as hosted in the public GitHub repository raffg/Coursera-Data-Analysis-and-Interpretation.

import pandas as pd
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use("Agg")
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import scipy.stats as stats

data = pd.read_csv("data/gapminder.csv", na_values=" ")
cols = ["incomeperperson", "lifeexpectancy", "internetuserate", "urbanrate"]
data[cols] = data[cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors="coerce")
sub = data.dropna(subset=cols).copy()
print("countries with all four variables:", len(sub))
print(sub[["urbanrate", "lifeexpectancy"]].describe().round(2))

The points rise from the lower left to the upper right, so the relationship is positive and roughly linear. The cloud is wide: at a given urban share, life expectancy can differ by 20 years or more, especially in countries with 20 to 60 percent urban population.

The correlation

r, p = stats.pearsonr(sub["urbanrate"], sub["lifeexpectancy"])
print("Pearson r =", round(r, 4))
print("p-value =", p)
print("r squared =", round(r ** 2, 4))

Output:

Pearson r = 0.6149
p-value = 2.2527658903232717e-19
r squared = 0.3781

What this says in plain words.

  • r = 0.61, a moderately strong positive correlation. Countries with a higher urban share tend to have higher life expectancy.
  • The p-value is about 2e-19, far below 0.05, so we reject H0. The relationship is not a chance pattern in this sample.
  • r squared = 0.378. About 38% of the variation in life expectancy between countries is described by differences in urbanization. The other 62% is left to other things, such as income and health care.

Limits. This is an association between countries, not proof that moving to cities extends life. Richer countries tend to be both more urban and healthier, so income could be driving both. The next post checks exactly that with a moderator.
