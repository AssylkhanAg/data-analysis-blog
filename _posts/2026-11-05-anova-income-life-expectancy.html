--- layout: post title: "Does national income go with life expectancy? An ANOVA with Tukey post hoc tests" date: 2026-11-05 09:00:00 +0500 ---

This is part of a small series of posts that uses one data set, the Gapminder country data from the Data Analysis Tools course (213 countries, one row per country). The series asks how a country's income, its urbanization, its internet use and its life expectancy fit together. This post runs an analysis of variance.

Question and variables

Question: does average life expectancy differ between countries with low, middle and high income per person?

  • Response (quantitative): lifeexpectancy, life expectancy at birth in years.
  • Explanatory (categorical, 3 levels): income group, made by cutting incomeperperson (income per person, constant 2000 US dollars) into three equal-sized groups (tertiles): low, middle, high.

Hypotheses. H0: the mean life expectancy is the same in all three income groups. H1: at least one group mean is different. Significance level: 0.05.

Load and prepare the data

The first step loads the file and keeps the countries that have all four series variables (income, life expectancy, internet use, urbanization), so that the same 173 countries are used in every post of the series. The file is the Gapminder course file as hosted in the public GitHub repository raffg/Coursera-Data-Analysis-and-Interpretation.

The boxplot already shows a clear staircase: richer groups have higher life expectancy, and the spread shrinks as income rises.

The ANOVA

model = smf.ols("lifeexpectancy ~ C(income_group)", data=sub).fit()
print(sm.stats.anova_lm(model, typ=1))

Output:

                    df       sum_sq     mean_sq           F        PR(>F)
C(income_group)    2.0  9115.608041  4557.80402  112.390098  7.905928e-32
Residual         170.0  6894.083177    40.55343         NaN           NaN

What this says in plain words. The F statistic is 112.4 and the p-value is about 8e-32, far below 0.05. So we reject the hypothesis that the three groups have the same average life expectancy. At least one group differs.

One caution: the standard deviations are not equal (7.6 years in the low group, 4.7 in the high group), which ANOVA assumes. With a gap this large I do not expect the conclusion to change, but the p-value should not be read as exact.

Post hoc: which groups differ?

A significant ANOVA only says that some difference exists. Because there are three groups, I ran Tukey's HSD test, which compares every pair while keeping the overall chance of a false positive at 5%.

print(pairwise_tukeyhsd(sub["lifeexpectancy"], sub["income_group"].astype(str)))

Output:

Interpretation. All three pairs are significantly different (adjusted p below 0.001, and the confidence intervals do not include zero). Countries in the high income group live on average 17.6 years longer than countries in the low group, 6.7 years longer than the middle group, and the middle group lives 10.8 years longer than the low group. The gap between low and middle income is bigger than between middle and high.

Limits. This shows an association between income level and life expectancy across countries, not that income causes longer life. The groups are also my own choice of cut points (tertiles).
